    abstract_en: 'Brazilian Modern architecture until 1960 is a myth: Brazilian architecture of the 1960’s is a mystery. What has happened after Brasilia and before the Post Brasilia critical revisions (1975)? Despite the intensive architecture production, with several important radical and high quality modern works, that moment still remains obscure in Brazilian and in international architectural historiography. Without revising it there will always be a missing chapter that would elude a comprehensive view of the present. This paper aims to analyze the reasons of such absence, and to try to increase the recognition of Brazilian architecture of the 1960’s which, even if including other trends, has had in the Paulista Brutalism one of its most important attributes. The inauguration of Brasilia and the wearing down of the Carioca School set up the birth of that trend in the end of the 1950’s, synchronizing with the world-wide brutalist trend, but with its own characteristics, related to the local and the international cultural panorama of that moment.'
